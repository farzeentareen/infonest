Essential Packing Checklist for Any Trip

Alex Morgan
July 15, 2026
6 min read
🎒

The optimal packing strategy is not "bring everything you might need", it's identifying the minimum you actually need and packing that. The traveler with a light bag moves faster, pays no checked luggage fees, never waits at baggage claim, and navigates airports and cities far more easily than one dragging an overpacked suitcase.

The Core Principle: Pack for a Week, Travel for a Month

Experienced travelers know that packing for a multi-week trip looks almost identical to packing for a week, because laundry is available everywhere. The goal is to bring versatile pieces that can be worn multiple times, in multiple combinations, and washed when needed. This applies particularly to clothing.

Documents and Money

  • Passport (check expiry, many countries require 6 months validity beyond travel dates)
  • Visa documents (if applicable, research requirements well in advance)
  • Flight booking confirmations (screenshot or print, don't rely solely on internet access)
  • Accommodation confirmations
  • Travel insurance documents and emergency contact numbers
  • Credit/debit cards (notify your bank before travel; bring at least two different cards)
  • Some local currency in cash for arrival (ATMs at destination airports are almost always available but may have high fees)
  • Photocopies/photos of all documents stored separately from originals

Clothing (One-Week Capsule)

This varies by destination, season, and purpose, but a functional baseline for most trips:

  • 5-7 pairs of underwear and socks (merino wool is ideal, odor-resistant and quick-drying)
  • 3-4 T-shirts or tops in neutral colors that coordinate with each other
  • 1-2 long-sleeve shirts or light layers
  • 1 pair of trousers/pants that work for walking and a casual dinner
  • 1 pair of shorts or casual trousers (climate-dependent)
  • 1 versatile jacket (packable down jacket or light rain jacket depending on climate)
  • One pair of comfortable walking shoes that look reasonable for an evening out
  • Flip-flops or sandals (for hostels, beaches, or warm climates)

Electronics

  • Smartphone and charging cable
  • Universal power adapter (one good one covers most destinations)
  • Portable battery pack
  • Camera or extra smartphone if desired (most modern smartphones are sufficient for travel photography)
  • Earphones or headphones
  • Laptop or tablet (only if needed, adds weight and value at risk)

Health and Toiletries

  • Prescription medications (with documentation; bring more than you need)
  • Basic OTC medications: pain reliever, antihistamine, antidiarrheal, rehydration sachets
  • Sunscreen (much more expensive at destination in many countries)
  • Insect repellent (essential for tropical destinations)
  • Hand sanitizer
  • Small first aid kit (plasters/bandaids, antiseptic wipes, blister plasters)
  • Travel-size toiletries (or plan to buy at destination, shampoo, etc. are universally available and often cheaper)
  • Toothbrush and dental care

Useful Extras

  • Day bag or packable daypack for exploring
  • Padlock (for hostel lockers)
  • Eye mask and earplugs (for overnight transport and noisy accommodations)
  • Reusable water bottle
  • Small notebook (for maps, recommendations, budget tracking)
  • Zip-lock bags (useful for leaking toiletries, wet swimwear, or organizing cables)

What Not to Pack

More valuable than what to pack is knowing what not to pack. Leave behind: multiple books (get an e-reader or use library apps if you read a lot), more than one pair of dress shoes, more than 2-3 days of clothing if you're traveling for longer than a week, anything you'd be devastated to lose, and anything you're bringing "just in case" for a scenario that probably won't happen. When in doubt, leave it out, the things you don't pack won't slow you down, and most things you discover you need can be bought at your destination.
